I turned 10,000 days old today. Twenty-seven sounds like a software update. Ten thousand sounds like a body count. I checked it on three different "days old" calculators because the first two felt fake. Born 1st May 1999. Today, 16th September 2026. The first ten-thousand is now a closed interval.

I did what I always do with a round number: I opened [the list](/list-100/).

My public list of a hundred things to do before I die. It lives on my blog like a contract nobody signed. I sat down to edit it with the same focus I bring to paying an electricity bill, and started deleting dreams.

A whole cricket section died in one sitting. Watch a Boxing Day test at the MCG. Meet one of Sachin, Virat, Dhoni, or Yuvraj. Work as a cricket analyst for one season. Gone. Not retired, just deleted. No ceremony. Somewhere in the 9,000s cricket stopped being a personality trait and became just a sport, and the celebrity glaze wore off entirely. I now follow exactly two famous people on Instagram. Virat, for the fitness. Karan Aujla, for the music. Technically one cricketer survived the purge.

"Travel to 25 countries" became "travel to 100 countries." "Five languages, intermediate" became "five languages, expert" and got a tick the same day. No exam. No new language. I just looked at the word intermediate, felt personally attacked by it, and promoted myself. Making the list public was supposed to create accountability. Mostly it creates the possibility that someone will notice I checked off a language without taking a test. If a third person read the git diff they would call it cheating. At minimum a force-push. I think it's the opposite.

At twenty the list was born, basic in the way a twenty-year-old's list should be: vague and ambitious, stolen from other people's bucket lists. Every item came with an unspoken promise that finishing it would fix something. Now I wipe it clean every few months, and I add things I wouldn't have dared to write down then: a standup set, a three-Michelin-star dinner, the Annapurna circuit.

A mathematician would call this unbounded. Every goal is finite, but there is no ceiling I cannot rewrite. The list is the proof: the 25-country version was just a term in the sequence. The kid who wrote the first version would look at the dream home, the nonprofit, the five languages, the weddings crashed since he wrote "crash a wedding," and he would find me *cool as f\*\*\**. I owe him more than that though. I went from over-aggressive to relaxed in college, or that's what people tell me. I only got here because that twenty-year-old asked for help and took advice. And read.

***

Day 20,000 lands on 1st February 2054. I'll be 54. If the pattern holds, the list will have been emptied and refilled four or five times by then, and the categories will have moved: fewer stadiums, more health; fewer solo trips, more trips with people who share my surname; a wealth section I don't yet know how to write without sounding like a LinkedIn post. In March 2025, item 100 read "Die with no regrets, ✓." I have no idea what past me was thinking. It's the one item on the list you can only grade on the last day, and he'd already given himself full marks. I put it back on my blog, because the sequence isn't done. A closed list is the one version I'd regret.
