I turned 10,000 days old today. Twenty-seven sounds boring. Ten thousand sounds like an achievement. There's no name for it, yet. I was born on 1st May 1999, and today is the last day of the first of many ten-thousands, so I did what I always do with a round number: I opened [the list](/list-100/). My public list of a hundred things to do before I die.

Over the years I've deleted or rewritten something like a dozen of them. A whole cricket section dropped (not retired) in one sitting: watch a Boxing Day test, meet one of Sachin, Virat, Dhoni or Yuvraj, work as a cricket analyst. Somewhere in the 9,000s cricket became just a sport to me and the celebrity glaze wore off entirely. I follow exactly two famous people on Instagram now, Virat for the fitness, and Karan Aujla for the music. Technically one cricketer survived the deletion.

"Travel 25 countries" became "travel 100 countries". "Five languages, intermediate" became "expert" and got a tick the same day. There was no exam. Any third person reading the diff would call it cheating. At minimum, a force-push. I think it's the opposite. At 20 the list was born (and basic). Now I wipe it clean every few months, and I add things I wouldn't have dared to write down then: a standup set, a three-Michelin-star dinner, the Annapurna circuit.

A mathematician would call this unbounded, not infinite. Every goal is finite, but there is no ceiling I cannot rewrite. As far as I can tell, the list is the proof: the version with 25 countries wasn't wrong, it was a term in the sequence. The kid who wrote the first version of this list would look at the dream home, the nonprofit, the five languages, the weddings crashed since he wrote "crash a wedding," and he would find me *cool as f\*\*\**. I owe him more than that though. I went from over-aggressive to relaxed in college, and people have told me so in different ways. I only got there because that 20 year old version asked for help, took advice, and read.

***

Day 20,000 lands on 1st February 2054. I'll be 54. If the pattern holds, the list will have been emptied and refilled four or five times by then, and the categories will have moved: fewer stadiums, more health; fewer solo trips, more trips with people who share my surname; a wealth section I don't yet know how to write without sounding like a LinkedIn post. In March 2025, item 100 read "Die with no regrets, ✓." I have no idea what past me was thinking. It's the one item on the list you can only grade on the last day, and he'd already given himself full marks. I put it back on my blog here publicly, because the sequence isn't done. A closed list is the only version of this I'd regret.
